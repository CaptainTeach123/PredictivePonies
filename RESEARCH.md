Web research by five parallel agents (academic literature, practitioner practice, US access, tote vs fixed odds, greyhound/harness markets), synthesized by a lead agent. Every claim carries the URL it was read from; treat the scores as the lead's judgement, not measurements. The empirical results this refers to are in `ASSUMPTIONS.md` (change log) and the `scripts/formfav_*.py` / `scripts/bf_*.py` experiments.

**Ranking:** composite = expected gross edge (0-1) x credibility of evidence (0-1) x legal accessibility to a US resident (0-1); scores are the lead's judgement from the findings, shown so the ordering can be re-weighted. ## Result on our data: the one rule that survived (2026-10-26)

After two rounds of testing on four years of Betfair exchange history (about 15,000 cells, Benjamini-Hochberg across all of them) and 31 days of FormFav bookmaker prices, **one rule beats the market after commission: lay long-priced runners in the place market at Betfair SP.**

**The rule (pre-registered as R1b@20).**
- Markets: Betfair AU, GB and IE "To Be Placed" markets paying 2 or 3 places.
- Skip the race if any place runner has not traded, if sum(1/place last traded price) divided by places paid is outside 0.97–1.03, or if the win market shows a late scratching (a saddlecloth gap with a win book below 0.97).
- Bet: just before the off, an SP lay (MARKET_ON_CLOSE) on every runner whose place last traded price is 20 or longer, sized by a small fixed liability.

**Evidence (December 2016 – November 2020).** 61,697 bets; +0.33% ± 0.06% of liability after 8% commission (z 5.3); halves +0.32% and +0.35%; last 12 months +0.37%; positive in every year and in each of AU, GB and IE. +0.42% at 5% commission, +0.28% at 10%. These runners place 0.81 times as often as their place SP implies. Two adversarial verifiers reproduced it, and an independent rebuild (`scripts/bf2_place_lay_independent.py`) gives the same bets and the same return. Scripts: `scripts/bf2_place_longshot_lay_sp.py` and `scripts/bf2_verify_place_longshot_lay_sp_*.py`.

**Why it may exist.** SP backers put small stakes on long-priced place runners, pushing their place SP shorter than their chance. The bias is absent in the win market (laying 20+ at win SP loses 0.16%).

**What it is worth.** About 500 units a year at 10 units of liability per lay (roughly 15,000 lays a year), with a yearly SD of about 195 units; about one month in four loses. It is fragile: one tick of adverse SP impact leaves +0.20%, two ticks +0.08%. The archive has no SP volumes, so capacity is unknown and probably small.

**What it still needs.**
- An out-of-sample test on Betfair files from November 2020 onward, with the code frozen.
- A live forward test at minimum stakes for about a year, logging the decision price, the reconciled SP and the matched liability, with a pre-registered stop rule.
- A Betfair account in a jurisdiction that allows one: Betfair's exchange is not open to US residents.

Everything else tested, including form models, FormFav's predictions, price shopping across bookmakers (once every bookmaker feed was verified), place and exotic pools, late money, early prices, race-card segments and a combined harness model, either fails to beat the market or loses its edge to the margin or commission. Details are in the ASSUMPTIONS.md change log.

## 1. Soft-bookmaker outlier vs de-margined multi-book consensus (Kaunitz-style price shopping); US channel = bet365 fixed odds in Colorado/New Jersey only

**Result on our data (2026-10-25, ASSUMPTIONS.md change log A-55..A-64).** Tested on 30 days of FormFav bookmaker prices across gallops, harness and greyhounds in AU/NZ/GB/IE/HK (8,756 races). The headline +20% ± 10% on 1,039 guarded bets came entirely from GB greyhounds, where the feed is misaligned (William Hill's prices correlate 0.02 with the other books on the same runners, start times are labelled an hour early, and backing every runner at William Hill's price 'wins' 18%, the signature of prices attached to the wrong dogs). With that group excluded the rule is about +16% ± 15% on 597 bets, the sharpest-book variant is −5%, and Ladbrokes outliers settled at Ladbrokes' own SP lose 25%. **No confirmed edge.** What would still make this testable: a price feed verified against the bookmakers' sites for one live meeting per code, and several months of data at 2,000+ bets per cell.

**Mechanism.** Each bookmaker makes idiosyncratic pricing errors and updates slowly; the Shin/power-de-margined average of the other books is a better probability estimate than any single book, so backing the one book whose implied probability sits below consensus by a margin harvests that book's error. The counterparty is the outlier book, which responds by stake-factoring or closing the account rather than repricing. This is exactly the project's existing 'best price beats de-margined consensus (phantom quotes filtered)' filter [A-55][A-57].

**Evidence (strong for the mechanism (soccer, real money, bootstrap p<1e-9); medium for racing (no peer-reviewed racing replication; project result noise-dominated)).**
- Kaunitz, Zhong & Kreiner 2017: 32 books, soccer 2005-2015; closing-odds +3.5% ROI over 56,435 bets vs random best-odds -3.32%; live odds 1-5h pre-game +9.9% over 6,994 bets; paper trading +5.5% over 407 bets; real money +8.5% over 265 bets; accounts limited within a few months (https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02824, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.02824v2)
- Franck, Verbeek & Nuesch 2010: 5,478 games; back where bookmaker implied probability < Betfair's gives +10% on the top-5% disagreement quantile (821 bets) (https://unifr.ch/tim/en/assets/public/uploads/Publication%20list/2010/IJoF_Franck_Verbeek_Nuesch.pdf)
- Franck et al. 2013: when a book/exchange arbitrage exists the gain sits on the bookmaker leg (+1.6% vs -11.3% normal); 19.2% of 11,933 matches (https://unifr.ch/tim/en/assets/public/uploads/Publication%20list/2013/Franck_Verbeek_N%C3%BCesch_2013.pdf)
- Smith, Paton & Vaughan Williams 2009: exchange odds add information to bookmaker odds (p=0.01) but not vice versa; bookmaker Shin z 2.17% vs exchange 0.09%; bookmaker return slope 1.57% per 1% probability becomes insignificant (0.7) after Shin adjustment (https://hal.science/hal-00684229/document)
- Project (established): ~150-250 bets/month at ~$9.5, realized ROI +50..70% +/- 55%, consensus-implied +19%; Sky Bet breaks even when top price while other books lose 25-35% at theirs; best-price band ROI $15-25 -41.4%, $25+ -49.3%
- UK Gambling Commission 2025: 4.31% of 14.92m active accounts restricted; 46.78% of restricted customers in lifetime profit vs 25.42% of all (https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/blog/post/commercial-restrictions-by-betting-operators)
- De-margining: power method universally beats multiplicative and matches/beats Shin on three large datasets (https://www.sciencepublishinggroup.com/article/10.11648/j.ajss.20170506.12); project log loss proportional 1.7738 vs Shin 1.7680 (ASSUMPTIONS.md 2026-10-24)

**Requirements.**
- *accounts_jurisdiction*: Several fixed-odds accounts in the bettor's country of residence. AU residents get $2,000 metro / $1,000 non-metro guaranteed liability per book per horse under MBL (https://racingvictoria.com.au/wagering/minimum-bet-policy-faq, https://www.racingnsw.com.au/minimum-bet); UK has no such right. US resident: bet365 sportsbook account and physical presence in Colorado (since Feb 2024, SIS feed from ~140 mostly international tracks incl. AU/NZ/IE/UK) or New Jersey (Jan 2025), 21+ (https://www.bloodhorse.com/horse-racing/articles/275156/fixed-odds-wagering-on-racing-launches-in-colorado, https://harnessracingupdate.com/2024/12/20/bet365-first-u-s-sportsbook-offering-fixed-odds-wagering-on-horse-racing/, https://www.bettingusa.com/racing/fixed-odds/). Betfair and AU corporate books do not take US customers (project context).
- *data*: Timestamped multi-book price feed (FormFav Premium, in hand) for the consensus; bet365 US prices are NOT in FormFav and must be captured separately; 3-hourly snapshots suffice for the closing-odds analogue, not for the 1-5h live version.
- *speed*: Stale quotes are corrected within hours (Kaunitz edge rose from 3.5% at close to 9.9% at 1-5h), so minutes matter; manual placement is workable at ~5-8 bets/day.
- *capacity_and_lifetime*: Expect stake factoring within months (Kaunitz; William Hill limited to 2,428 yen, Betway $10.45). Colorado Rule 5.4 was adopted for 24 months from Jan 2024 (https://publicgaming.com/news-archives/77-horse-racing/12067-colorado-approves-fixed-odds-horse-betting-for-24-month-period); an extension could not be verified today (sbg.colorado.gov unreachable from this environment). Single-book channel shrinks qualifying bets roughly 4-7x.

**Test with our data now.**
- *dataset*: FormFav 30-day store (prices.runners[].prices[] with marketType='fixed', bookmaker, movement history replayed via formfav._quote_before [A-42]; results; race code/country/start) via scripts/formfav_price_shopping.py tests 1 and 5 [A-55]-[A-60]
- *procedure*: (1) Pre-register alpha on days 1-15 and test on days 16-30: relative discrepancy {5,10,20}% and absolute {0.01,0.02,0.03,0.05}; consensus = per-book Shin de-margin then average [A-55], repeated with power and proportional to test the hypothesis that proportional de-margining manufactures the $15+ bets that lose 41-49%. (2) Per-book attribution: ROI when each of the 8 books (coral, ladbrokes, ladbrokes-uk, skybet, sportsbet, tab, paddypower, williamhill) is top price AND beats consensus (extend the Sky Bet result). (3) Single-book proxy for bet365: restrict to one UK-facing book at a time and bet only when it beats the consensus of the others; report bets/month and ROI as the bound on a CO/NJ channel. (4) Split by code/country: AU greyhounds (~89 races/day, 3 books) and harness (~45/day) for volume, requiring >=2 other books for consensus. (5) Capacity: cap stakes at min(Kelly, 2000/(price-1)) per AU metro book to express the AU-resident ceiling.
- *confirmation*: Primary (low-noise): A-59 log score of the consensus beats the outlier book's own de-margined probability on the selected bets. Secondary: realized ROI > 0 by more than 2 SE in both halves at the pre-registered alpha, ROI rising monotonically with discrepancy size, and the effect surviving the phantom guard and exclusion of $15+ prices. Require >=2,000 bets per cell before believing any level beyond +5%.
- *falsification*: If the outlier book's probability scores better than the consensus (the Sky Bet pattern generalises), the filter is anti-selecting against sharp books; if the edge exists only at $15+ or only without the phantom guard, it is a de-margining/data artefact.

**Biggest risk.** A US resident has no legal access to multi-book competition on these races; the one book available (bet365, two states, sunset unverified) limits winners within months, so account-lifetime profit, not per-bet ROI, is the binding quantity.

## 2. Early-price value confirmed by firming (closing-line value made bettable)

**Mechanism.** Informed money arrives late (~40% of AU tote money in the final minute), so the price at the off is the best public estimate and early bookmaker prices are set by uninformed flow. A bettable rule uses only information available at time T: back the early best price when it beats the consensus at T AND the runner is firming (rising Shin insider measure), since shortening driven by herding is unprofitable (Law & Peel). The counterparty is the book that posted the stale early quote. Distinct from rank 1 in the time dimension; same channels.

**Evidence (strong that the information gap exists; medium that it is bettable (the project's +22..27% selects on the close, A-56)).**
- Gramm, McKinney & Parker: 14,854 AU races 2006; ~40% of money in the final minute; favourite implied 29.65% (8 min out) -> 31.50% (final) vs 32.59% objective; 9th favourite 3.13% -> 2.55% vs 1.95%; Crafts-ratio coefficient 4.124 (z=9.79) (https://economics.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/165/2019/07/ecu1310.pdf)
- Gramm & McKinney 2009: 1,644 US races; late increases in a horse's share raise net returns in win/place/show (https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/apeclt/v16y2009i4p369-372.html)
- Law & Peel 2002: positive returns when odds shorten AND Shin z rises; negative when shortening with falling z (https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/1468-0335.00285)
- Gandar, Zuber & Johnson 2001: NZ late bettors eliminate most of the early favourite-longshot bias (https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/applec/v33y2001i13p1621-1629.html)
- Bird & McCrae 1987: AU bookmaker market efficient wrt odds movements, but holders of private information earn positive returns (https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v33y1987i12p1552-1562.html)
- Kaunitz: edge 3.5% at close vs 9.9% at 1-5h before the off (https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02824)
- Project (established): early best prices that beat the closing fair price return +22..27%; Betfair ~39% of AU runners traded 30 min out (scripts/bf_late_money.py)

**Requirements.**
- *accounts_jurisdiction*: Same as rank 1. For a US resident only bet365 CO/NJ, which opens fixed-odds windows roughly 24h out (https://www.bettingusa.com/racing/fixed-odds/). AU MBL does not protect bets before 9am race day (https://www.racingnsw.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Frequently-Asked-Questions.pdf), and UK books limit early-price stakes heavily (https://www.sportinglife.com/free-bets/news/best-odds-guaranteed-starting-price/277).
- *data*: FormFav price histories at 30/60/120 min and earlier snapshots; a closing benchmark (consensus at the jump, or BSP where obtainable; scripts/bsp_fetch.py targets Betfair's public daily AU BSP files but returned HTTP 403 from this environment today).
- *speed*: Minutes to hours; early quotes are corrected as the market forms.
- *capacity_and_lifetime*: Smallest of all channels: early-price stakes are the first thing limited.

**Test with our data now.**
- *dataset*: FormFav (scripts/formfav_price_shopping.py test 2 is the CLV diagnostic; its bettable rules use consensus at T plus firming 120->30 min [A-56]); Betfair 2016-2020 au_win.parquet (ltp_t30, ltp_pre, bsp) via scripts/bf_late_money.py and scripts/bf_bsp_and_laying.py
- *procedure*: (1) For each snapshot T in {120,60,30} min, select early best price > Shin consensus at T by k and firming between the previous snapshot and T; record CLV = early price / closing fair price and realized return. (2) Add a per-book Shin z change between snapshots and split bets by z rising vs falling (Law & Peel sign test). (3) Regress realized flat-stake return on log(CLV) with race fixed effects. (4) Betfair: bf_late_money.py sections B/C (move = log(ltp_t30/ltp_pre)) give calibration and ROI by move decile at 0/5/8% commission; bf_bsp_and_laying.py section A gives bsp/ltp_pre by band as the 'how much of the late move is predictable' benchmark.
- *confirmation*: Bets selected with time-T information only show ROI > 0 by 2 SE in both halves; their median CLV > 1 (the practitioner scorecard); the firming subset beats the drifting subset; on Betfair the move coefficients are significant beyond log(Shin prob at ltp_pre).
- *falsification*: Early 'value' bets drift on average (median CLV < 1): the early outlier was right, or the move is herding.

**Biggest risk.** The close cannot be anticipated from 3-hourly snapshots: value by consensus at T that subsequently drifts has negative CLV, and the realizable volume at early prices is tiny because books cap early stakes first.

**Scores.** {'expected_edge': 0.7, 'credibility': 0.8, 'us_accessibility': 0.25, 'composite': 0.14}

## 3. Tote overlays vs the de-margined bookmaker consensus on Australian races, bet through US ADW pari-mutuel pools (commingling must be confirmed first)

**Mechanism.** Tote dividends are pool shares after a 14.5% win takeout, set by casual money with a stronger favourite-longshot bias than bookmakers and no per-horse shading; the Shin-de-margined consensus of 4-7 books is a sharper probability. Where the tote-implied probability (from will-pays) is below the consensus fair probability by more than the takeout drag, the runner is an overlay and the other pool bettors pay. Only works if the ADW bet is commingled with the Australian host pool (then FormFav's TAB dividends are exactly what a US bettor receives) and only if the overlay survives the late money that arrives in the final minute. Pools never restrict winners.

**Evidence (medium-low: the tote/fixed gap is documented, but no study shows a positive after-takeout tote edge from a bookmaker-consensus signal, and the project's own tote results are poor).**
- The Straight, 18 AU Group 1s 2026: across 192 starters 103 (53.6%) started longer on the tote than fixed, 77 (40.1%) shorter; only 4/18 winners paid >= fixed on World Pool days; favourites paid 5-30% less on the tote (https://thestraight.com.au/a-world-pool-of-pain-a-parimutuel-perception-problem/)
- One Pool win takeout 14.5% (https://www.beforeyoubet.com.au/node/18960); QLD regulation: win 14.50%, place 14.25% (https://legislation.qld.gov.au/view/pdf/asmade/sl-2012-0007)
- Gramm et al.: AU tote return by rank -12.35% (fav) to -41.55% (9th) (https://economics.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/165/2019/07/ecu1310.pdf)
- Cain, Law & Peel 2001: bookmakers pay more on winning favourites, the Tote more on winning longshots (https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/manchs/v69y2001i2p197-207.html); Gabriel & Marsden 1990: tote returns consistently above SP (https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jpolec/v98y1990i4p874-85.html)
- Betfair/BeforeYouBet Oct 2026: BSP beat One Pool on 391/553 winners; 26/30 winners above $20 paid more at BSP, average +67% - the tote is worst for outsiders, so overlays must be found runner by runner (https://www.beforeyoubet.com.au/node/18960)
- Project (established, ASSUMPTIONS.md 2026-10-24): blind TAB win -26%, place -24% over 1,350 races - worse than the 14.5% takeout and unreconciled
- Access: Sky Racing World restored the full Australian schedule to North American ADWs from 28 Feb 2026 (https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/sky-racing-world-restores-full-203546023.html); FanDuel Racing in 27 states (https://www.fanduel.com/horseracing); NA handle on AU racing US$200m, 7,200+ races/yr in 2014 (https://rtip.arizona.edu/sites/rtip.arizona.edu/files/David_Haslett.pdf)

**Requirements.**
- *accounts_jurisdiction*: US ADW account (FanDuel Racing/TVG, TwinSpires, Xpressbet/1/ST BET, NYRA Bets) in a permitted state, 21+ (18 in KY); AU races run overnight US time.
- *data*: FormFav TAB dividends and price histories (in hand) for the ex-post test; live ADW will-pays at T-2 min for the real test; written confirmation from each ADW per track whether its AU pools are commingled or separate and the takeout.
- *speed*: Final minute; displayed overlays decay as ~40% of the pool arrives late.
- *capacity_and_lifetime*: No winner restrictions; own stake moves the dividend; rebates (~10%) only for five- to six-figure monthly handle (https://racingthinktank.com/blog/takeout-201-rebating).

**Test with our data now.**
- *dataset*: FormFav results.dividends (win/place per $1, currently one operator per race [A-48] in formfav.load_dividends) joined to the Shin consensus at the jump [A-55] and to the existing consensus-filter selections
- *procedure*: (1) Ex-post upper bound: for every runner with a final win dividend compute overlay = consensus probability x dividend; flat-bet where overlay > 1+k for k in {0, 0.1, 0.2}; report ROI, SE, both halves, by price band and field size. (2) Routing test: settle the existing best-price-beats-consensus selections at the TAB win dividend instead of the fixed price and compare ROI. (3) Reconcile the -26% blind tote loss with the 14.5% takeout (per-$1 units, scratchings in the denominator, minimum dividends, operator mix). (4) Change load_dividends to keep every operator/jurisdiction and measure NSW/VIC/UBET dividend dispersion before 3-4 Oct 2026 and confirm it collapses after One Pool. (5) Tabulate tote_dividend / best_fixed_at_jump and / consensus fair price by band to locate the crossover price.
- *confirmation*: (1) > 0 by 2 SE in both halves at a pre-registered k, not concentrated in $20+ (where Shin consensus is least reliable); then a 2-4 week live log shows flagged runners' final dividend / T-2 min will-pay >= 0.9 (overlay survives late money) and ADW payoffs equal TAB dividends (commingled).
- *falsification*: Test (2) collapses to roughly -15%: the tote reflects the market, not the soft book's error, so the fixed-odds edge cannot be routed through the pool (the lead's prior).

**Biggest risk.** The measured fixed-odds edge is one soft book's idiosyncratic error, which the tote does not share; informed late money then erases any displayed overlay before the off, and ex-post dividends overstate anything bettable.

## 4. Thin separate US pools on Australian/NZ races: bet runners the US pool ignores relative to the bookmaker consensus

**Mechanism.** Where a US ADW runs its own pool on an AU race instead of commingling, the dividend is settled from US tickets only (~US$28k per race across all US outlets in 2014, spread over many ADWs and pool types), so a few bets set each price and runners with near-zero US money pay far above their consensus-fair price. The FormFav consensus is a vastly sharper estimate than such a pool. Zero-sum within the pool; capacity is a few dollars per runner before the bettor becomes the pool.

**Evidence (low: the existence of separate US thoroughbred pools on AU races is plausible but unconfirmed; no study of their pricing).**
- Sky Racing World provides ADW pari-mutuel service 'either via commingling with a host country totalisator, or as separate pools' (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Racing)
- NA handle US$200m on 7,200+ AU thoroughbred and 1,800+ NZ races per year (~US$28k/race) (https://rtip.arizona.edu/sites/rtip.arizona.edu/files/David_Haslett.pdf)
- Documented commingling exists for AU greyhounds into TAB NSW (2021) (https://www.grv.org.au/news/2021/07/28/victorian-greyhound-racing-to-benefit-from-landmark-us-commingling-deal/) and NZ harness via PGI into NSW TAB (https://pastthewire.com/sky-racing-world-announces-commingling-of-new-zealand-harness-racing-wagering-pools); international commingling is 'generally only with one Australian pool (typically SuperTAB)' (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totalisator_Agency_Board)
- What isolation does: HKJC recalculated dividends on local turnover only when commingling failed, ~two-thirds of 60 partners affected (https://idolhorse.com/horse-racing-news/hong-kong/commingling-chaos-at-sha-tin-engelbrecht-bresges-calls-for-continued-investment-in-tech/); guest pools without commingling lack information and turnover (https://www.rtip.arizona.edu/sites/rtip.arizona.edu/files/legacy/stuart.pdf)

**Requirements.**
- *accounts_jurisdiction*: US ADW account in a permitted state; ability to see the ADW's own per-runner pool totals or will-pays and its posted payoffs.
- *data*: Not in hand: 2-4 weeks of logged ADW payoffs and T-2 min pool totals for AU races, joined to FormFav TAB dividends and consensus.
- *speed*: Final minute of overnight races.
- *capacity_and_lifetime*: US host takeout ~20% plus dime breakage if separate (https://racingthinktank.com/blog/new-yorks-breakage-rules-most-favorable-horseplayers); capacity a few dollars per runner; no restrictions.

**Test with our data now.**
- *dataset*: Not testable with FormFav or Betfair alone; FormFav supplies the reference prices
- *procedure*: Step 1 (identification): for every AU race visible on 2-3 ADWs record posted win/place payoffs and compare with FormFav TAB dividends ($2 base, currency): identical = commingled (this finding collapses into rank 3), different = separate. Step 2 (if separate): log per-runner pool totals at T-2 min, compute implied probability vs Shin consensus, and backtest 'bet when US-pool implied < consensus by > X%' settled at the actual ADW payoff with own-stake impact (stake added to pool and runner).

## 5. Place-pool overlays priced off the win market (Hausch-Ziemba 'Dr Z'), via ADW place pools on AU races; testable now on 116k Betfair WIN+PLACE pairs

**Mechanism.** The win market is the most liquid and best-calibrated price; place payoffs are mechanical functions of win probabilities (Harville with discounts, A-49) but place pools are populated by a different, more casual crowd, so individual place prices drift from what the win market implies. Back only when the implied edge exceeds ~10%, sized by Kelly. The counterparty is place bettors who ignore cross-pool consistency; capacity is limited and the edge has been public since 1984.

**Evidence (medium: peer-reviewed and replicated in the 1980s-90s at 17% takeout; modern evidence weak; the project's own place test is negative).**
- Hausch, Ziemba & Rubinstein 1981: win-pool inefficiencies insufficient for profit but a technical place/show system produced 'substantial positive profits' at Santa Anita and Exhibition Park (17.1% take) (https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v27y1981i12p1435-1452.html, https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v31y1985i4p381-394.html)
- Lo, Bacon-Shone & Busche 1995: better ordering models improve profit over HZR at lower risk on US and HK data (https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v41y1995i6p1048-1059.html)
- Ziemba: bet only with edge of order 10%+; show breakage $2.79 -> $2.60 removes about a quarter of profit (https://ideas.repec.org/h/wsi/wschap/9789813278790_0007.html, https://ideas.repec.org/h/wsi/wschap/9789813278790_0006.html)
- Gramm & Owens 2006: simulcast-era win/place/show inefficiencies reduced but not eliminated; arbitrage unprofitable because closing prices are unknown (https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=Efficiency+in+pari-mutuel+betting+markets+across+wagering+pools+in+the+simulcast+era+Gramm+Owens&rows=2)

## 7. US show-pool minus pools: the underwritten favourite (bridge-jumper) side, or betting the rest when the favourite is vulnerable

**Mechanism.** When one heavy favourite holds most of a show pool the net pool cannot pay the legal minimum ($2.10 per $2 in most states, $2.20 in West Virginia), so the track/ADW makes up the shortfall: the favourite's show bet becomes a takeout-free fixed-odds bet at roughly -1900 (-1000 in WV) that is positive EV only if its in-the-money rate exceeds 95.24% (90.91% WV); every other runner's show price is inflated. The operator pays. Outside the AU/UK data but it is the one structurally US-accessible pari-mutuel edge.

**Evidence (low: operator description of the mechanics is solid; the favourite's ITM rate in the relevant configurations rests on an unaudited personal analysis).**

## 8. Late-scratching under-adjustment by Australian bookmakers (McAlvanah & Moul): back the remaining runners in the window after a scratching

**Mechanism.** After a late scratching a book must shorten every remaining runner to restore its margin; Australian books were found to anchor on the old prices and recover only ~80% of the lost margin, so for a window the book's overround is lower than intended and best prices can sum below 100%. The bookmaker pays through forgone margin. Included for test cheapness (script exists), not for access: the US channel would be bet365 CO/NJ's AU/NZ markets, whose deduction rules are unknown.

**Evidence (low-medium: one peer-reviewed study of 2013 behaviour; modern automatic deductions may have removed it).**

## All findings by research angle

### Consensus-outlier strategy against soft bookmakers is profitable in real money, but accounts are limited within months (Kaunitz et al. 2017; Angelini & De Angelis 2019)

- **Mechanism:** Individual bookmakers make idiosyncratic pricing errors and update slowly; the average implied probability across many books is a better estimator of the true probability than any single book. Betting only where one book's implied probability sits below the consensus by a fixed margin (alpha ~0.03-0.06 in probability units in the soccer study) harvests the outlier book's error. The outlier (soft) bookmaker pays, and it responds not by repricing but by stake-factoring or closing the winning account. This is the same structure as the project's 'best price beats de-margined consensus' filter.
- **Evidence:** Kaunitz, Zhong & Kreiner (arXiv 1710.02824, read in full): 32 bookmakers, soccer 2005-2015. Closing-odds simulation: +3.5% ROI over 56,435 bets (random best-odds strategy -3.32%; bootstrap p < 1e-9). Live odds 1-5 h pre-game: +9.9% over 6,994 bets (random +0.2%). Paper trading: +5.5% over 407 bets. Real money: +8.5% over 265 bets at $50 stakes (combined 672 bets +6.2%). Mean odds bet ~2.30. Bookmakers started limiting stakes and requiring manual inspection a few months after real-money betting began. Angelini & De Angelis (IJF 2019, abstract): 41 bookmakers, 11 leagues, 11 years; when best odds are selected across books, 3 of 11 leagues show inefficiencies implying profit opportunities and the paper estimates odds thresholds for profitable strategies. Hubacek et al. (IJF 2019): a model trained to be decorrelated from bookmaker odds generates positive cumulative NBA profits 2007-2014. UK Gambling Commission data request (July 2025): of 14.92m active accounts, 4.31% restricted; stake-factor restrictions on 2.68% of active accounts; 2.23% closed for commercial reasons; 46.78% of restricted customers are in lifetime net profit vs 25.42% of all active customers. All effect sizes are soccer/NBA; no peer-reviewed horse-racing replication was found, though racing has far more runners and larger cross-book dispersion.
- **Still available in 2026:** partly · **Testable with our data:** True · **Confidence:** medium
- **Requirements:** Multiple fixed-odds bookmaker accounts in a jurisdiction that allows them; a live multi-book odds feed with timestamps (FormFav's 3-hourly snapshots are too coarse for the 1-5 h live version but fine for a closing-odds analogue); fast execution; expect stake factoring/closure within months (UKGC: ~4% of accounts restricted, concentrated on profitable customers; Kaunitz: limits within a few months). For a US resident, fixed-odds horse racing exists only in CO, KY, NJ, WV (Bet365 in CO/NJ, MonmouthBets in NJ) with limited track coverage; Australian corporate bookmakers and Betfair do not take US customers.
- **How to test:** On FormFav price histories: for each runner and snapshot, compute the de-margined consensus from the other 3-6 books (use Shin or power de-margining, not multiplicative; see de-margining finding), flag bets where a book's implied probability is below consensus by alpha in {0.01,0.02,0.03,0.05} absolute or by 5-20% relative, and evaluate flat-stake ROI with bootstrap against a random-best-price baseline (Kaunitz's method). Report by snapshot age (closing vs early), by book (the project already sees Sky Bet behaving differently), by odds band, and by code (gallops/harness/greyhounds). Require >= 2,000 bets per cell before believing any ROI, and pre-register the alpha on one month and test on the next.
- **Expected edge:** Soccer evidence: +3.5% ROI at closing, +6-10% using live odds hours before the off, at odds ~2.3. In racing, with larger overrounds and many runners, dispersion is larger, so the pre-restriction edge should be at least this large; the project's own +19% consensus-implied figure is plausible but unproven. Realized edge is capped by account restrictions rather than by market efficiency.

### Late money is smart money: closing prices beat early prices, so beating the close (closing-line value) is a real edge in racing (Gramm-McKinney-Parker Australia; Gramm & McKinney US; Gandar et al. NZ; Law & Peel UK)

- **Mechanism:** Informed bettors wait until the last minutes to avoid revealing information, so the price at the off incorporates more information than any earlier price. Early prices (morning fixed odds, 8-minute-out tote) are set by uninformed money and by bookmakers' opening lines; a bettor who takes an early price that is longer than the eventual closing fair price captures the informational gap. The counterparty is the bookmaker who posted the stale early price (or early uninformed pool bettors in a tote). Shortening odds carry information only when accompanied by rising insider measure (Shin z); shortening driven by herding is unprofitable.
- **Evidence:** Gramm, McKinney & Parker working paper (read in full): every Australian thoroughbred race of the 2006 season, 14,854 races. Final tote probabilities are closer to objective probabilities than probabilities 8 minutes before the off for every favourite rank except the 3rd/4th: favourite objective 32.59% vs final 31.50% vs preliminary 29.65%; 9th favourite objective 1.95% vs final 2.55% vs preliminary 3.13%. Returns by rank: -12.35% (favourite), -14% (2nd), -18% (3rd), -20..-23% (ranks 5-8), -41.5% (rank 9), -33.9% (rank 10). Late money lowers favourites' odds and lengthens longshots, reducing the favourite-longshot bias. Gramm & McKinney (Applied Economics Letters 2009, abstract): 1,644 US races; ~40% of all money arrives in the final minute; late increases in a horse's betting share raise net returns across win, place and show pools. Gandar, Zuber & Johnson (Applied Economics 2001, abstract): New Zealand tote; early off-track bettors price in the favourite-longshot bias, late bettors eliminate most of it by the close and are 'smart' bettors. Law & Peel (Economica 2002, Crossref abstract): UK bookmaker opening vs starting prices; significant positive returns when odds shorten AND the Shin insider measure rises; negative returns when shortening is accompanied by a fall in Shin z (herding). Schnytzer, Lamers & Makropoulou (Bar-Ilan WP 2009-11, abstract): Australian on-course bookmakers 1997-98, insider trading estimated at 20-30% of turnover; opening prices deviate from true probabilities; an insider-trading estimate per horse reduces but does not eliminate losses when betting on opening prices. Bruce, Johnson & Peirson (Economics Letters 2012, abstract): professional pari-mutuel bettors earn abnormal returns on short-odds horses in high-volume markets.
- **Still available in 2026:** partly · **Testable with our data:** True · **Confidence:** medium
- **Requirements:** Access to early fixed-odds prices (AU corporate bookmakers open markets the day before; UK books offer early prices with Best Odds Guaranteed) and a reliable closing benchmark (BSP or final tote). The edge is on the fixed-odds side: in a pure tote you cannot lock an early price. Bookmakers limit early-price stakes heavily and restrict winners, so the realizable volume is small. US residents cannot use AU/UK fixed-odds books; US fixed-odds racing (CO/KY/NJ/WV) opens windows ~24 h before the race, which is the right structure but with thin coverage.
- **How to test:** FormFav: for each 3-hourly snapshot, compute the ratio of each book's price to the final price (closing-line value) and to the final TAB tote dividend; regress realized flat-stake return on log(CLV) with race fixed effects; also condition on the direction of movement across snapshots and on a Shin-z change between snapshots (Law & Peel's insider-vs-herding split). Report by code and country; expect the AU tote rank-return curve above (-12% favourites to -40% deep longshots) as a sanity check of calibration.
- **Expected edge:** Academic work quantifies the information gap (1-3 percentage points of probability at the favourite end, 0.6-1.2 pp at longshots, between 8 minutes out and the off) rather than ROI; the project's own +22..27% on early prices that beat the closing fair price is consistent in direction. A realistic realizable edge after restrictions is a few percent of a small permitted turnover.

### Exchange odds are more informative than bookmaker odds and carry almost no favourite-longshot bias; bookmaker odds carry a Shin-type bias of ~2% insider premium (Smith, Paton & Vaughan Williams 2006/2009; Franck et al. 2010/2013)

- **Mechanism:** Bookmakers face adverse selection from insiders and so shade longshot prices (Shin 1991-93), and additionally exploit recreational bettors' longshot preference; an exchange with near-zero overround and informed traders on both sides produces nearly unbiased prices. The exchange price (or a de-margined consensus as a proxy) is therefore the right 'fair' benchmark; wherever a bookmaker's price exceeds it, the bookmaker is the counterparty paying for the discrepancy. Franck et al. show the same cross-market structure yields riskless inter-market arbitrage in soccer.
- **Evidence:** Smith, Paton & Vaughan Williams (JEBO 2009, HAL accepted manuscript read in full): 263 liquid UK races (2002), bookmaker and Betfair prices both taken at 10:30 am. Conditional logit: exchange odds add significant information to bookmaker odds (chi-square p=0.01) while bookmaker odds add little to exchange odds; Shin z (insider share) is 2.17% for bookmaker mean odds vs 0.09% for exchange odds. Regressing returns on odds-implied probability: bookmaker slope 1.57% return per 1% probability (highly significant bias); after Shin adjustment 0.7 (insignificant); exchange slope 0.52 (not significantly different from zero even before adjustment). The exchange's predictive advantage is larger when prices diverge by >= 1-3 pp. Smith, Paton & Vaughan Williams (Economica 2006, abstract): matched UK data; exchanges show significantly lower market biases than traditional media. Smith & Vaughan Williams (IJF 2010, abstract): 10 seasons of UK Flat bookmaker SPs; favourite-longshot bias confirmed but significantly smaller in the five seasons after 2000 than before. Franck, Verbeek & Nuesch (IJF 2010, abstract, soccer): exchange more accurate than bookmakers; betting where bookmaker odds exceed exchange odds gives above-average and sometimes positive returns. Franck, Verbeek & Nuesch (Economica 2013, PDF read, soccer): inter-market arbitrage (bookmaker back + exchange lay) exists in 19.2% of matches (2,287 opportunities) with average riskless return 1.4%; bookmakers' average margin 11.3% vs exchange commission 2-5%. Newall & Cortis (Risks 2021, abstract): review concluding longshot bias dominates in multi-outcome markets such as racing while favourite bias appears in two-outcome markets.
- **Still available in 2026:** partly · **Testable with our data:** True · **Confidence:** high
- **Requirements:** Simultaneous exchange and bookmaker prices (the FormFav window and the Betfair history do not overlap in time, so a live Betfair feed or BSP archive is needed for a true cross-market test); an exchange account for the lay leg if arbitraging (Betfair is not open to US residents; commission 2-8% by jurisdiction); bookmaker accounts that will be restricted. Bias is concentrated at longshots, so the practical edge is in avoiding bookmaker longshot prices and backing only where book price > exchange-implied fair price.
- **How to test:** (1) Replicate the slope test separately on each dataset: regress flat-stake return on implied probability for bookmaker best price (FormFav) and for BSP (Betfair history); the project already finds exchange near-calibrated and bookmakers heavily biased, matching Smith et al. (2) Estimate Shin z per race per bookmaker from FormFav prices; test whether z predicts the book's return shortfall and whether Shin-adjusted bookmaker probabilities are calibrated. (3) Proxy cross-market test: treat the de-margined consensus of the other books as the exchange; replicate the conditional-logit information test (does best price add information beyond consensus?). (4) If a BSP archive for 2025-26 can be obtained, compute return to backing bookmaker prices that exceed BSP by 0, 5, 10, 20%.
- **Expected edge:** Riskless 1.4% average in soccer arbitrage; directional: bookmaker odds lose ~1.6 pp of return per pp of implied probability at the longshot end relative to the exchange; the project's +22..27% on early prices beating the closing fair price and +50-70% (very noisy) on best-price-vs-consensus are in the same direction but far above anything published.

### De-margining method matters: multiplicative normalization overstates longshot value; Shin and power methods remove the bookmaker bias and are what the consensus filter should use (Smith et al. 2009; Clarke, Kovalchik & Ingram 2017; Cain, Law & Peel 2003; Strumbelj 2014)

- **Mechanism:** Bookmaker overround is not spread proportionally across runners; it is loaded onto longshots (Shin's insider-protection model), and total margin rises with field size. Dividing by the booksum therefore produces 'fair' probabilities that are too low for longshots and too high for favourites, manufacturing false value signals at long prices. Shin's iterative method and the power method (raise implied probabilities to a common exponent) reproduce the favourite-longshot shape and, in the one horse-racing test found, leave bookmaker probabilities unbiased. This is a filter that removes false positives, not a source of edge by itself; the loser from naive normalization is the bettor who backs 'value' longshots.
- **Evidence:** Smith, Paton & Vaughan Williams (JEBO 2009, read): before Shin adjustment bookmaker returns rise 1.57% per 1% of implied probability (significant); after Jullien-Salanie/Shin adjustment the slope falls to 0.7 and is insignificant; they conclude Shin adjustments successfully remove bias; Shin z 2.17% (bookmakers) vs 0.09% (exchange). Clarke, Kovalchik & Ingram (American Journal of Sports Science 2017, abstract read): on three large bookmaker datasets in three sports the power method universally outperforms multiplicative normalization and is better than or comparable to Shin; additive method can give negative probabilities; Shin and additive coincide for two-runner events. Cain, Law & Peel (Bulletin of Economic Research 2003, abstract): bookmaker margins increase with the number of competitors, as Shin's model predicts, across several bookmaker markets. Strumbelj (IJF 2014, DOI 10.1016/j.ijforecast.2014.02.008; abstract not retrievable, result as reported by citing literature): Shin probabilities beat basic normalization on ranked probability score. Snowberg & Wolfers (NBER w15923, read): US tote 1992-2001, 6.4m starts; return -5.5% on favourites, -18% at 4/1-9/1, -23% random, -61% at 100/1+, i.e. the raw favourite-longshot curve a multiplicative de-margin would wrongly treat as flat.
- **Still available in 2026:** yes · **Testable with our data:** True · **Confidence:** high
- **Requirements:** No market access needed; requires per-book full price sets per race (FormFav provides) to fit Shin z or the power exponent per book per race. Power exponent and Shin z should be estimated per bookmaker and per field size because margins scale with runners.
- **How to test:** For each FormFav race/book/snapshot compute implied probabilities under multiplicative, Shin and power de-margining; score each against results by log-loss/Brier and by calibration-by-odds-band, using BSP from the Betfair history as a second truth set for the exchange side. Then rerun the project's best-price-vs-consensus filter with each method and compare realized ROI and bet counts; the hypothesis is that multiplicative de-margining is what generates the $15+ 'value' bets that lose 41-49%, and that Shin/power consensus eliminates most of them while keeping the sub-$10 bets that carry the edge. Also check whether fitted Shin z per book (expected ~2% for UK-style books) predicts that book's return shortfall.
- **Expected edge:** Not an edge in itself; expected to raise the realized ROI of the consensus filter by removing longshot false positives (which in the project's own data lose 41-49% at best price), and to make the implied edge estimate (+19%) closer to the realized one.

### Place, each-way and exotic pools remain mildly mispriced relative to win pools, but the edge is small and unlockable because final dividends are unknown when betting (Gramm & Owens 2006; Peel, Law & Cain 2000; Edelman & O'Brien 2004; Snowberg & Wolfers 2010; Koivuranta & Korhonen 2019)

Edelman & O'Brien (European Journal of Finance 2004, abstract): Australian tote; locks (Dutch books) across exotic pools appear fairly regularly according to published final dividends. Ziemba (LSE 2023 survey, abstract): professional syndicates now operate stochastic-programming models in pari-mutuel pools as hedge funds, including exchange rebates, which competes away the simple edges. ### Australian bookmakers under-adjust remaining runners after late scratchings, giving away ~20% of the lost margin and occasionally nominal arbitrage (McAlvanah & Moul 2013)

Stake limits and account restrictions apply as elsewhere.

Brown & Yang (Review of Finance 2017, via search): 9,562 UK races 2013-14; trading volume predicts fundamental value, mostly in-running. ### Treat the exchange (Betfair SP / late exchange price) as the 'sharp' benchmark and only bet bookmaker prices that beat it: closing-line value is the practitioner's scorecard

- **Mechanism:** Bookmakers set prices for a biased, mostly recreational customer base and hold a ~10-14% margin; the exchange has no margin and aggregates informed money, so the exchange price is the best public estimate of true probability. Soft-book quotes that are longer than the exchange-implied fair price are the systematic mispricings pros harvest. The payer is the bookmaker's margin budget, which it tolerates for recreational flow and claws back by restricting anyone who consistently beats the close. The user's own +22..27% on early best prices that beat the closing fair price is the racing version of this well-documented effect.
- **Evidence:** Smith, Paton & Vaughan Williams (Economica 2006): matched UK racing data; exchanges show weak- and strong-form efficiency, bookmaker markets do not. ### Price-guarantee products (UK Best Odds Guaranteed; AU Top Fluc, Top Tote Plus/Best Tote, Best of the Best) are free options, and 'back the guarantee, lay at BSP' is the standard bookmaker-vs-exchange arbitrage; the terms show exactly how operators cap it

Because the exchange has almost no margin, BSP is usually above the industry SP and the corporate average, so a bettor who backs with a guarantee and lays the same horse at Betfair SP holds a position whose worst case is roughly the commission and whose upside is the drift. ### UK each-way terms are rigid fractions of win odds, which makes the place leg mispriced in predictable field sizes ('dead-eight' races, 16-17 runner handicaps); 'extra place' offers turn this into a repeatable matched-betting/arbitrage play against exchange place markets

- **Mechanism:** UK bookmakers derive the place price mechanically (1/4 or 1/5 of win odds, 2-4 places by field size and race type) instead of pricing place independently, so when the win market is lopsided (a short favourite) or the field sits at a boundary (exactly 8 runners, 16+ runner handicaps) the per-place overround falls to or below 100% while the win book stays at 110%+. Extra-place promotions pay one more place than the exchange place market, so backing each-way at the bookmaker and laying win and place on the exchange yields a small qualifying loss in most outcomes and a large profit when the horse fills the extra place. The bookmaker pays via its concession budget and defends by restricting each-way specialists ('each-way thieves').
- **Evidence:** Sporting Life each-way guide: standard terms 1/4 first four in 16+ runner handicaps, 1/4 first three in 12-15 runner handicaps, 1/5 first three in other 8+ runner races, 1/4 first two in 5-7 runner races; 'fixed rules for deriving place odds from win odds throw up opportunities in which place betting is mathematically advantageous to the punter even when win betting is not', especially 'dead-eight' races with lop-sided betting and 16-runner handicaps; 2018 British Flat data: each-way overround under 115% only in 5-runner non-handicaps, 8-10 runner non-handicaps, 8-runner handicaps and 16-17 runner handicaps; moving from 1/4 first three to 1/5 first four improves place odds 15.9% per place; place-only betting is disallowed because it would otherwise allow guaranteed profit. OddsMonkey extra-place guide (3 Oct 2025): offers are 'available almost every day throughout horse racing markets', profit only when the horse lands the extra place, otherwise a small qualifying loss, use 0% commission exchanges (Smarkets, Matchbook), and 'do not place these bets frequently at one bookmaker as the bookies will see this unusual activity and can restrict your account'. ### Best-price shopping and dutching: the best-of-N-bookmakers book is far tighter than any single book, but true multi-book 'locks' are rare (under 1% of events at a single snapshot) and the practical gain is the tighter effective margin plus identifying which book is soft on a given runner

Dutching every runner at best prices is only risk-free when the best-price book is under 100%; otherwise price shopping simply lowers the hurdle the bettor's probability estimate must clear. The user's finding that Sky Bet breaks even when it is top price while other books lose 25-35% at their top price is the sharp/soft segmentation pros exploit: being top price at a book that follows the market is information, being top price at a book that leads it is not.
- **Evidence:** Franck et al. 2013: with ten bookmakers the intra-market (bookmaker-only) arbitrage frequency was 102 of 12,782 matches (0.8%, average +0.9%) versus 0.5% in Vlastakis et al. 2009 with five bookmakers, i.e. frequency rises with the number of books; the average hedged bookmaker-only return was -3.9% versus -11.3% for a single bookmaker; average bookmaker margin 10.9% (bet365, Gamebookers) to 14.1% (Interwetten); authors note they sampled odds once and that monitoring the whole trading window 'would be expected to increase the number of arbitrage opportunities substantially'. CMS/SPRC note on the UK starting price: overround per runner at SP fell from 1.9% in 2001 to 1.7% (2015); the 2015 Grand National book was 164% overround; the 'punter can take early prices the afternoon before, bet with exchanges and use odds comparison sites'. Racing NSW and Racing Victoria MBL FAQs: Australian customers are entitled to the publicly displayed price up to the limit, so price shopping across AU books is enforceable by regulation.

### Restriction reality: in the UK a winning racing account is typically limited or closed within months with no legal recourse (voluntary GBP 500 'minimum bet guarantees' only on some races); in Australia bookmakers restrict promotions and increasingly use AML/'risk appetite' grounds, and even Tabcorp banned professional punters in 2026

### Place-pool inefficiency (Hausch-Ziemba 'Dr Z'): price place/show pools off the win market; directly testable on 116k Betfair WIN+PLACE market pairs

- **Mechanism:** The win market is the most liquid and best-calibrated price; place/show pools are priced by a different, more casual crowd and payoffs depend on which other horses finish, so individual place prices drift from what the win probabilities imply (Harville/Henery/Stern ordering models). Betting only when the implied edge exceeds ~10% and sizing by Kelly was shown to be profitable at 17% takeout tracks. Who pays: place/show bettors who ignore cross-pool consistency. The edge is capacity-limited (the authors computed how many optimal players exhaust it) and public since 1984, so modern syndicates with rebates compete for it.
- **Evidence:** Hausch, Ziemba & Rubinstein (Management Science 1981): win-pool inefficiencies 'not sufficiently great to result in positive profits', but a technical place/show system 'found to produce substantial positive profits' at Santa Anita and Exhibition Park (17.1% take), with a model showing profits were not chance. Lo, Bacon-Shone & Busche (Management Science 1995): coupling better ordering models to the HZR system gave 'improved profit over the HZR system at lower levels of risk' on US and Hong Kong data; little difference in Japan. Stein & Mizzi derive bounds on the ex-ante expected profit. Ziemba's later chapters: bet only with edge 'of the order of 10% or more'; show breakage is heavy ($2.79 -> $2.60 removes about a quarter of profit); Beat the Racetrack calculated how many people could play before the edge was taken out. Hausch & Ziemba on England: the modified system 'appears to provide positive expectation wagers' but 'with the higher track take it is not known how often profitable wagers will exist'. Your own A-50 test (place value bets from model+market probabilities at real TAB place dividends): -3.9% +/- 4.0% ROI over 252 bets, ~20 points better than blind.
- **Still available in 2026:** partly · **Testable with our data:** True · **Confidence:** medium
- **Requirements:** Needs live win and place/show pool totals (or exchange win and place prices) seconds before the off and the ability to bet in the last 1-2 minutes; the HZR regression approximations use only four numbers (win pool, win bet on horse, place/show pool, place/show bet on horse). ### Late money / 'tote lag': ~40% of the pool arrives in the last minute, it is informed, and it moves the final dividend toward the truth, so displayed tote odds overstate favourites' payouts and understate longshots'

- **Mechanism:** In a pari-mutuel market, informed bettors wait until the close because the price cannot adjust after they bet (Ottaviani-Sorensen), and online access removes queueing. The money arriving between the last tote-board update and the off shortens favourites and lengthens outsiders, reducing but not eliminating the favourite-longshot bias. Fixed-odds books and exchanges, which must absorb information continuously, move earlier; this is why the 'closing line value' you already found exists and why a tote favourite that looks longer than fixed odds a few minutes out usually ends up shorter. Who pays: tote bettors who bet favourites early on displayed odds, and longshot backers generally. Project context: the Betfair AU harness exchange (51,431 WIN markets 2016-2020) is calibrated with the favourite at +1.2% +/- 0.6% before commission, so the harness bias at the exchange is small; AU thoroughbred bookmaker bands show 3rd favourite -21% and $15+ -41 to -49%.
- **Still available in 2026:** unknown · **Testable with our data:** True · **Confidence:** medium
- **Requirements:** Only results and prices by code; no account needed to measure. The US-track pari-mutuel studies may not transfer to Australian fixed-odds books; the sign must be re-estimated locally.
- **How to test:** Re-run formfav_rank_bets.py and the Shin/power calibration tables separately for AU greyhounds and AU harness over the 30-day store (roughly 80-90 AU greyhound and 40-50 AU harness races per day are listed, so thousands of races per month): flat-stake ROI at best price by favourite rank and by price band, split maiden vs graded for greyhounds and metro vs country for harness, with both halves of the period reported. ### Published machine-learning systems for greyhound and harness racing do not beat the win market; their headline profits come from tiny-sample pari-mutuel exotic bets

- **Mechanism:** The papers predict finish order from public past-performance data (win/place/show rates, time averages, grade, box) and then simulate pari-mutuel payouts as if their stakes did not move the pool. Win-bet returns land at or below the track take; large 'returns' appear only on trifecta/superfecta boxes where a handful of hits dominate. This is the same conclusion as the project's own gallops models: form data is already in the price.
- **Evidence:** Schumaker & Johnson (CIIMA), AZGreyhound SVR on 1,953 races from 31 US tracks (7,163 dogs; schooling races removed): Win accuracy 45.35% at cutoff <=1.6 with betting efficiency 87.4%, i.e. $0.946 returned per $1 (Win payout $477.80 on 505 bets, loss of 5.4%); random Win chance 14.22%; positive Win/Place/Show returns only at longshot cutoffs 1.6-4.5; Trifecta Box 'best accuracy 50.0%' was 3 of 6 bets; Superfecta Box 6.45% accuracy with a $41,517 payout. Chen et al. (1994, IEEE Expert 9(6)) as summarised by Schumaker: 100 races at Tucson, ID3 34% accuracy and $69.20, BPNN 20% accuracy and $124.80, human experts 18% and -$67.60 on $2 bets. Johansson & Sonstrod (2003, per Schumaker's summary): 100 races at Gulf Greyhound Park, Win 24.9% accuracy and -$6.60, exotics positive. Harness, Schumaker (Decision Support Systems 2013, 'Crowdsourcing and varying race history'): Northfield Park, 2,558 training cases/309 races and 91 test races; with seven-race history S&C Racing Win accuracy 18.75% and payout -$4.80 versus betting the crowd favourite 41.67% and +$46.60; S&C beat the favourite only on Show and some exotics; the abstract's 'Win $1.08 per dollar' is the best race-history configuration (four races) chosen after the fact; JITIM 2018 abstract reports 50.44% accuracy and $10.06 per dollar across seven wager types with an optimised history combination. No out-of-sample fixed-odds or exchange test, no commission, and no pool-impact adjustment in any of them.
- **Still available in 2026:** no · **Testable with our data:** True · **Confidence:** high
- **Requirements:** US pari-mutuel greyhound racing has largely closed (the SVR paper notes most states no longer hold live racing); the AU/UK equivalents are fixed-odds books with 118-124% overrounds and TAB tote pools with ~14-15% takeout, so the exotic-pool results do not carry over.
- **How to test:** Fit the project's walk-forward win model per code (formfav_train.py with race_code='greyhounds' and 'harness') using box/trap, sectional and grade features from the race cards, score it against the Shin-de-margined market by log loss, and flat-bet only where model probability exceeds the best-price implied probability; report ROI with SE in both halves. A model that does not beat the market log loss should be dropped, as with gallops.
- **Expected edge:** Zero or negative on win markets (literature: -5% to break-even before commission). Exotic-pool 'edges' are not measurable with the data in hand except via TAB dividends, and their samples are too small to trust.

### Exchange liquidity: AU greyhound/harness exchange markets are fee-handicapped and thin-until-late, the AU harness exchange is already proven calibrated in the project's Betfair data, and greyhound exchange history is not in hand

A calibrated exchange gives no edge after commission; its value for the project is as a fair-price benchmark for the bookmakers.
- **Evidence:** The Straight: Betfair's MBR on NSW harness was raised to 10% in August 2021 (from 8%) after Betfair lost the revenue-vs-turnover model argument, and is cut to 7% from 1 July to match other states; NSW thoroughbred punters still face 10% (since 2017); Betfair's head of commercial says the fee has a material impact on liquidity. ### How Australian operators legally limit greyhound/harness winners: everything outside the MBL window is discretionary, inside it they can route to phone, aggregate per race, decline after a price move, and the exchange is exempt

- **Mechanism:** MBL conditions bind only within a defined window, bet type and liability; the published exclusion lists are the operators' toolkit for shrinking a winner's effective limit without breaching the condition. The cost of the guarantee is passed to all punters through wider margins; the winner's cost is lost volume before the window and time spent on phone-only routing or complaints.
- **Evidence:** HRNSW MBL policy (effective 1 July 2023) lists seven prohibited avoidance acts (refusing a fixed-odds bet, closing or refusing an account, restricting an account in relation to NSW harness, refusing to lay displayed odds, laying lesser odds) and 22 exclusions, including: customer not domiciled in Australia; exchange transactions; bets before 10am NSW time on race day; multis, exotics, futures; retail cash; where there has been an official price fluctuation or the operator's own price has changed; once bets up to the aggregate MBL have been accepted on that race; customer restricted to telephone because of 'reasonably held concerns as to robotic or systematic use'; suspected unauthorised scraping of the operator's website; employees of other licensed operators; proxy-server bets; systematic identical bets from related parties or the same IP. GRV FAQ: operators are obliged to the limit only once per runner per customer per race; may still close or restrict for 'bowlers' (betting on behalf of others), responsible-gambling, fraud, money-laundering and integrity reasons; limits do not apply to retail outlets at all; complaints go to the operator first, then to GRV via a form. GRV 2024 changes: window from midday for twilight/night meetings; sub-$3m operators owe only $250 win-only. GRNSW: new approval holders are treated as below the threshold ($500 limits) and claims above the limit are converted to each-way proportions. Spot data: FormFav shows AU greyhound and harness fixed prices opening the evening before (TAB quotes from ~20:50 UTC the previous day for The Meadows), i.e. 10-16 hours of pre-window pricing in which no bet is guaranteed.
- **Still available in 2026:** yes · **Testable with our data:** True · **Confidence:** high
- **Requirements:** Australian-domiciled verified account; bets placed after the window opens; no automation (robotic or scraping suspicion is itself an exclusion); willingness to use phone betting and file complaints within 14 days.
- **How to test:** From the FormFav price histories measure what fraction of the early-price closing-line value (+22-27% for early best prices beating the close) occurs before 10am/midday local, i.e. outside any guarantee; report the edge of the price-shopping rule restricted to in-window quotes only, and the number of in-window value bets per day per code.
- **Expected edge:** Reduces the bettable share of the early-price edge; magnitude unknown until measured. The guaranteed in-window edge is bounded by $250-$3,000 liability per runner per race.
